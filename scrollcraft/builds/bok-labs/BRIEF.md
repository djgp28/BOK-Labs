# BRIEF · bok-labs.com

Interviewed 2026-09-01/02 with David Govea (owner, BOK Labs) in Claude Code, in
three rounds of multiple-choice questions plus free answers. Answers marked
`interviewed` are his; answers marked `assumed` were proposed by the builder and
are recorded as such.

## The eight questions

1. **Vibe in three to five words, plus references.** `interviewed`:
   "Precise, calm, engineered." Premium-minimal, dark, lots of air, mono
   details, "like a well-made tool". References: none given. Keep the colours
   and the logo of the existing site.
2. **The scroll journey, in his words.** `interviewed` for the offer and the
   peak, `assumed` for the order: the visitor should recognise their own
   operation first (WhatsApp, spreadsheets, memory), then see what it costs,
   then watch the mess become a system, then see real systems BOK Labs has
   built, then be asked one thing: what is slowing you down.
3. **The energy curve.** `assumed`: calm open, rising unease, one line of
   silence, the peak, steady proof, settled close.
4. **How should someone feel, and the ONE moment.** `interviewed`: the one
   moment is "chaos becomes a system": scrolling untangles a mess of
   spreadsheets, WhatsApp threads and sticky notes into one clean flowing
   pipeline that keeps running on its own. Feelings per stage `assumed`, see
   the curve below.
5. **One thing this site should do that no site they have seen does.**
   `interviewed` (chosen from three proposals): the before/after is told by the
   scroll itself. The visitor's hand does the tidying.
6. **Distance from premium-minimal.** `interviewed`: premium-minimal. Dark,
   one green accent, air.
7. **One unbroken world or distinct scenes.** `interviewed`: distinct scenes.
8. **What assets exist.** `interviewed`: the brand kit of the current site
   (palette, Space Grotesk + JetBrains Mono, "BOK [labs]" wordmark and the
   green "[B]" tile). Media to be generated with kie.ai (his key, cap around
   150 credits, shot list approved by him before generation). Three live
   products to name as proof.

## Journey (six beats)

```
1  Recognition   their own desk: the chat, the spreadsheet, the sticky note
2  Tension       three costs, each answered by one quiet status line
3  Turn          one sentence, nothing else: "Then someone builds the system."
4  Substance     PEAK: the mess is pulled straight into one pipeline that keeps running
5  Proof         three real systems, named and linked, one real outcome each
6  Commitment    the divider collapses, the system wins, the form is the only thing left
```

## Feeling curve

```
1  Recognition   the cluttered desk moving under their hand, beside a calm surface they do not have yet
2  Unease        each cost on the left answered by a status line on the right; the divider leans toward the mess
3  Stillness     one line in the system column. Authored silence: the chaos column carries only a timestamp.
4  Relief        PEAK: chips fly across the divider and tidy themselves into nodes, the scribbles un-draw, the pipeline draws and starts running
5  Trust         three named systems with what they had on the left and what runs now on the right
6  Resolution    the chaos half collapses off the edge, the system takes the width, the form sits in it and holds
```

Authored silence: act 3 (flow, about half a viewport) is deliberately quiet.
The chaos column there carries one muted timestamp line, nothing else.

## The peak

Act 4. "I scrolled and the WhatsApp messages and spreadsheets on the left flew
across the line and lined themselves up into a system on the right, and then it
kept running by itself."

## Tell-someone sentence

It's the site where you scroll and the mess of chats and spreadsheets straightens
itself into one line that keeps running after you stop.

## Grammar

**Split stage.** Left column = today (chaos), right column = with a system.
The divider is the chrome: a fixed hairline that carries both labels, the page
progress, the wordmark and the language toggle. Hero at 50/50 with both
headlines readable at once. Close = the collapse: the divider travels to the
left edge and the form lives in the winning column.

Why the other seven lost: filmic one-shot hides the seam this brief is about
(and it is the AgerPRO build's grammar); live surface needs a real running
product on the page; typographic poster forbids the one clip the brief asked
for; continuous world has no geography here and is the most fragile build;
chaptered editorial reads as a manifesto, not a tool; gallery/catalog answers
"what are the options", not "should I believe you"; rhythmic cutlist is a
pulse, and the brief said calm.

## Signature move

"Chaos becomes a system." Seven real-markup artifacts (a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp
bubble, a sticky note, an email thread, a notebook page, a voice note, a receipt
photo) sit scattered and rotated on the chaos side under a tangle of scribbles.
Driven from the act's `--sc-p`, each chip travels across the divider, loses its
rotation and docks beside a node of the pipeline; the scribbles un-draw; the
pipeline draws itself; once the last chip lands, the pipeline starts running
(a moving dash and a runner dot) and keeps running while the reader stays.
The divider itself moves with the argument: 0.50 in the hero, 0.60 by the end
of the tension act (the mess wins ground), back to 0.40 across the peak (the
system wins ground), 0 at the close.
